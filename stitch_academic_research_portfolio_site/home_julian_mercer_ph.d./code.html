Julian Mercer, Ph.D.

Research Staff Member · Center for Machine Intelligence & Language Technologies

University Research Laboratory · Cambridge, MA

My research investigates the computational foundations of speech perception and statistical language modeling, with an emphasis on self-supervised acoustic representations under severe signal degradation. I develop algorithmic frameworks that balance structural parameter efficiency with expressive invariant representations, enabling robust natural language understanding across linguistically underrepresented and non-stationary acoustic environments.

Prior to joining CMILT, I completed my doctorate in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at MIT, where I explored sequence distillation dynamics in non-autoregressive neural transducers. My current methodological work integrates empirical learning bounds with open, reproducible software architectures designed for both high-throughput distributed clusters and constrained edge hardware.

Investigation · 02

Current Focus

During the 2024–2025 academic cycle, my group is investigating non-isometric representation drift in continuous multilingual representations. As foundation audio models ingest thousands of uncurated dialect hours, we frequently observe asymmetric topological collapse wherein minority phonological structures are erased during dense projection phases.

Through an NSF-supported initiative with the MIT Speech Communication Group, we are developing spectral bounds that formally constrain feature deformation across phonetic manifolds. Concurrently, we are finalizing the v0.8 kernel benchmarks for OpenAcoustic to guarantee deterministic multi-GPU training on heterogeneous clusters without loss of precision.

Selected Projects

Open-source implementations, research libraries, and system artifacts built for longevity and empirical auditability.

OpenAcoustic: Modular Speech Representations in PyTorch

v0.7.4 · Active

A modular research framework engineered for composable acoustic feature extraction, contrastive objective validation, and hardware-accelerated beam decoding. Decouples front-end signal conditioning from multi-GPU gradient synchronization via custom C++ extension bindings.

[GitHub Repository] • [Documentation] • Apache 2.0

Distill-Wav: Low-Latency Sequence Distillation for Streaming ASR

Artifact · 2024

Reference code for end-to-end knowledge distillation targeting Conformer transducers under rigorous real-time latency thresholds. Reduces causal acoustic transformer runtime memory by 64% while maintaining sub-1.2% character error rate degradation on LibriSpeech.
